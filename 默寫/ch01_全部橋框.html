Chapter 1 — every bridge and every box

Bridge 橋 = the high-school picture that the new word is sitting on.
Box 框 = the one line you recopy on a quiz. There are not fifty of them. Kittel Ch. 1 is these boxes, not the whole book.
橋＝這個新詞踩在哪個高中畫面。框＝小考要默寫的那一行。第一章就這些，不是整本 Kittel。

A · Bridges（概念橋）

Each row: high-school picture → the Ch. 1 name. If you cannot say the left column, the right column is just a vocabulary list.

Bridge 1
Wallpaper that repeats forever, specified by a tiny tile.
能無限重複的壁紙，用一小塊磁磚就能講完。
$$\longrightarrow\quad \text{crystal structure}=\text{lattice}\times\text{basis}$$
Bridge 2
Integer steps along three arrows: $n_1\mathbf a_1+n_2\mathbf a_2+n_3\mathbf a_3$.
沿三支箭各走整數步。
$$\longrightarrow\quad \text{lattice translation }\mathbf T$$
Bridge 3
Smallest tile that covers the wall with no leftover.
能鋪滿又不重複的最小一塊。
$$\longrightarrow\quad \text{primitive cell (exactly 1 lattice point)}$$
Bridge 4
Voronoi cell: region closer to this base station than to any other.
離這座基地台最近的勢力範圍。
$$\longrightarrow\quad \text{Wigner–Seitz cell}$$
Bridge 5
Which polygons tile the plane: triangles, squares, hexagons — not pentagons.
正多邊形密鋪：沒有正五邊形。
$$\longrightarrow\quad \text{5 Bravais lattices in 2D, 14 in 3D; no 5-fold axis}$$
Bridge 6
Intercept form of a line: $x/a+y/b=1$, then take reciprocals.
直線截距式再取倒數。
$$\longrightarrow\quad \text{Miller indices }(hkl)$$
Bridge 7
How many equal balls touch this ball; what fraction of the box is ball.
一顆球貼幾顆；球佔盒子幾成。
$$\longrightarrow\quad \mathrm{CN},\quad \eta=N\cdot\tfrac43\pi r^3/V_{\mathrm{cell}}$$
Bridge 8
Stacking oranges: third layer either back over the first ($ABAB$) or in the other hollow ($ABCABC$).
疊橘子：第三層回到第一層正上方，或換另一個凹洞。
$$\longrightarrow\quad \mathrm{hcp}\ (ABAB)\ \text{vs}\ \mathrm{fcc}\ (ABCABC)$$

B · Boxes（全部公式框）

Recopy the boxed line. Derivation lives in Unit 01; the quiz wants the box. All numbers: Kittel 8e Ch. 1 Table 2 / Fig. 8 / 13 / 18 / 21 unless noted.

1

The whole chapter

$$\boxed{\text{crystal structure}=\text{lattice}\times\text{basis}}$$ $$\mathbf T=n_1\mathbf a_1+n_2\mathbf a_2+n_3\mathbf a_3,\qquad n_i\in\mathbb Z$$

Kittel: lattice translation vectors; basis and the crystal structure. sc = simple cubic, bcc = body-centered cubic, fcc = face-centered cubic.

整章一句話。lattice＝怎麼重複；basis＝每格放什麼。
2

Primitive-cell volume

$$\boxed{V_c=\lvert\mathbf a_1\cdot(\mathbf a_2\times\mathbf a_3)\rvert}$$ Conventional cube volume is always $a^3$. Primitive volumes: sc $a^3$, bcc $\tfrac12 a^3$, fcc $\tfrac14 a^3$.

Kittel Table 2.

3

Counting atoms in the conventional cube

$$\boxed{\text{corner }=\tfrac18,\quad \text{face }=\tfrac12,\quad \text{body }=1}$$ $$ N_{\mathrm{sc}}=8\times\tfrac18=1,\quad N_{\mathrm{bcc}}=8\times\tfrac18+1=2,\quad N_{\mathrm{fcc}}=8\times\tfrac18+6\times\tfrac12=4 $$

Kittel: one lattice point per primitive cell; Table 2 “lattice points per cell”.

4

Contact (hard spheres) → $r$ in terms of $a$

$$ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{sc}:&\quad 2r=a\\ \mathrm{bcc}:&\quad 4r=\sqrt{3}\,a\\ \mathrm{fcc}:&\quad 4r=\sqrt{2}\,a \end{aligned}} $$ Equivalent nn distances (Kittel Table 2): $a$, $\frac{\sqrt3}{2}a$, $a/\sqrt2$.

Table 2 nn distance + footnote: packing = hard spheres. Draw the red line: edge / body diagonal / face diagonal.

5

Packing fraction

$$\boxed{\eta=\frac{N\cdot\frac43\pi r^3}{a^3}}$$ $$ \boxed{ \eta_{\mathrm{sc}}=\frac{\pi}{6}=0.524,\quad \eta_{\mathrm{bcc}}=\frac{\pi\sqrt3}{8}=0.680,\quad \eta_{\mathrm{fcc}}=\frac{\pi\sqrt2}{6}=0.740 } $$ fcc form $\pi\sqrt2/6$ is Kittel’s; same as $\pi/(3\sqrt2)$. hcp has the same $0.74$. Diamond $\approx0.34$.

Kittel Table 2; diamond §; hcp § (both close-packings occupy 0.74).

6

Coordination number

$$\boxed{\mathrm{CN}:\ \mathrm{sc}=6,\ \mathrm{bcc}=8,\ \mathrm{fcc}=\mathrm{hcp}=12,\ \mathrm{diamond}=4}$$

Table 2; diamond § (4 nn, tetrahedral).

7

Names of the three cubic lattices

$$ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{sc}&=\text{simple cubic}=\text{corners only}\\ \mathrm{bcc}&=\text{body-centered cubic}=\text{corners}+\text{body centre}\\ \mathrm{fcc}&=\text{face-centered cubic}=\text{corners}+\text{face centres} \end{aligned}} $$

Kittel Fig. 8 and Table 2. Only sc’s conventional cube is primitive.

sc 簡單立方；bcc 體心立方；fcc 面心立方。
8

fcc primitive vectors

$$ \boxed{ \mathbf a_1=\tfrac a2(\hat x+\hat y),\ \mathbf a_2=\tfrac a2(\hat y+\hat z),\ \mathbf a_3=\tfrac a2(\hat z+\hat x) } $$ Length $\frac a2\sqrt2$, angle $60^\circ$, $V=a^3/4$.

Kittel Fig. 11.

9

bcc primitive vectors

$$ \boxed{ \mathbf a_1=\tfrac a2(\hat x+\hat y-\hat z),\ \mathbf a_2=\tfrac a2(-\hat x+\hat y+\hat z),\ \mathbf a_3=\tfrac a2(\hat x-\hat y+\hat z) } $$ $V=a^3/2$. (Kittel Fig. 10; one common sign convention.)

Kittel Fig. 10.

10

Miller indices

$$\boxed{\text{intercepts}\ \to\ \text{reciprocals}\ \to\ \text{lowest integers}=(hkl)}$$ Example: intercepts $3,2,2$ → $(233)$. Intercepts $4,1,2$ → $(142)$. Parallel to an axis → that index is $0$. Direction $[uvw]$; family $\{hkl\}$.

Kittel § Indices of planes, Fig. 13–14.

11

Cubic interplanar spacing

$$\boxed{d_{hkl}=\frac{a}{\sqrt{h^2+k^2+l^2}}}$$ In the cubic system only: $[hkl]\perp(hkl)$.

Standard cubic geometry (used with Fig. 14). Not true in a general crystal system — Kittel states the cubic perpendicularity.

12

Density from the cube

$$\boxed{\rho=\frac{N\,M}{N_A a^3}}$$

Mass of $N$ atoms per conventional cube. Used with Table 2’s $N$.

13

CsCl is not bcc

$$\boxed{\mathrm{CsCl}=\text{simple cubic lattice}+\text{basis }\{\mathrm{Cs},\mathrm{Cl}\}}$$ Sites $000$ and $\tfrac12\tfrac12\tfrac12$ are different ions → not equivalent lattice points → not bcc. CN $=8$.

Kittel Fig. 18, § Cesium chloride.

14

NaCl

$$\boxed{\mathrm{NaCl}=\text{fcc lattice}+\text{basis }\{\mathrm{Na},\mathrm{Cl}\}}$$ 4 NaCl per conventional cube. CN $=6$.

Kittel § Sodium chloride, Fig. 15–17. Basis: one ion at $000$, the other at $\tfrac12\tfrac12\tfrac12$ along the body diagonal (or Cl at $000$, Na at $\tfrac12 00$, etc.).

15

Diamond / zincblende

$$\boxed{\text{diamond}=\text{fcc}+\{000,\ \tfrac14\tfrac14\tfrac14\}\ \text{(same atom)}}$$ Conventional cube: 8 atoms. CN $=4$. Packing $\approx0.34$. ZnS (zincblende): same geometry, two species.

Kittel Fig. 22–24.

16

Ideal hcp

$$\boxed{\left(\frac{c}{a}\right)_{\mathrm{ideal}}=\sqrt{\frac83}=1.633}$$ Stacking $ABAB\cdots$ (hcp) vs $ABCABC\cdots$ (fcc). Both: $\eta=0.74$, CN $=12$. Space lattice of hcp is simple hexagonal with a 2-atom basis.

Kittel Fig. 19–21. Second basis atom at $\tfrac23\mathbf a_1+\tfrac13\mathbf a_2+\tfrac12\mathbf a_3$.

17

Bravais count / no five-fold

$$\boxed{\text{2D: 5 types};\quad \text{3D: 14 types};\quad \text{allowed rotations }1,2,3,4,6}$$ Honeycomb (graphene) is not a 1-point Bravais lattice (two environments) → triangular lattice + 2-carbon basis.

Kittel Table 1; § Fundamental types of lattices.

C · What is not a Ch. 1 box

  • Reciprocal lattice $\mathbf b_i$, Bragg, $S_{\mathbf G}$ — Chapter 2.
  • Phonons — Ch. 4–5. Nearly-free electron / tight binding — Ch. 7.
  • The $a\times a\sqrt2$ bcc slice is a drawing aid, not a Kittel box (derived from Table 2 nn).
